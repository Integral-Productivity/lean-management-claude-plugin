# lean-management-claude-plugin Strategy

## Purpose

People who use AI to improve their work get finished answers instead of capability. The AI does the thinking, invents data, and leaves no record of its reasoning that a later person or session can find.

## Positioning

Route each problem to the lean method that fits its type, and coach it with the AI empowered: it gathers data, adds instrumentation, suggests from the available evidence, and runs follow-ups, but every commitment stays with the operator. Facts come first; analysis is labelled, with clear attribution to human or AI and a clear mark of which claims are verified.

## Users

**Primary:** Lean practitioner, anywhere in the org - anyone who uses lean to improve work, in whatever seat they hold that day: owning a problem, running a routine, or coaching others. They hire the plugin to apply the lean method that fits the work in front of them, with an AI that does the legwork while they commit.

## Boundaries

- No single score for a team or a person. A maturity assessment is an evidence report per practice, with sources; the team draws the conclusion.
- No coaching of non-lean methods. When Six Sigma DMAIC, OKRs, or an agile retrospective fits the problem better, the plugin names the method, says why, and hands off.
- No commitments made for the operator. The AI suggests from evidence; the holder decides and commits.
- No organization-specific wiring or private data in this public plugin. That belongs in a private overlay plugin.

_Resist a change when:_ it hides evidence (a claim with no fact, inference, or not-measured tag), makes a commitment for the operator, grades people with one number, or coaches a method outside lean.

## Key metrics

- **Reviews on time** - share of open A3s whose `next_review` date has not passed without a revision; measured from A3 headers in Git.
- **Origin coverage** - share of issues, decision records, and pull requests made from a countermeasure that carry an `Origin:` line; measured in Git.
- **Closed with a standard** - share of A3s closed each quarter that record an adopted standard; measured from A3 files in Git.
- **Routines held as planned** - share of planned management routines (stand-ups, A3 reviews, gemba walks) that took place; not measured yet.
- **Practice spread to another team** - count of Yokoten items adopted by a second team; not measured yet.

## Tracks

### Shared core

Routing by problem type, the common vocabulary, and the file formats (the A3 reference and its check) that every layer uses.

_Why it serves the approach:_ one router and one record format let each problem reach the method that fits and leave findable reasoning behind.

### Thinking

Solve one problem: A3, 5 Whys, root cause analysis, value stream mapping.

_Why it serves the approach:_ this is where the AI does the legwork and the operator commits, one PDCA cycle at a time.

### Management

The routines that surface problems and hold the gains: leader standard work, daily accountability, visual management, A3 reviews.

_Why it serves the approach:_ routines turn single fixes into kept standards and feed new problems to the Thinking layer.

### Transformation

Build capability and spread learning across teams: coaching, kaizen events, hoshin planning, Yokoten.

_Why it serves the approach:_ capability, not finished answers, is the purpose; this layer grows it beyond one team.
